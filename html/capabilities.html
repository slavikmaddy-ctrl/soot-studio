HOW A PICTURE GETS MADE

STEP 1 — PENCIL DAYS

Quick thumbnails tacked to the board, pinned and re-pinned until the gag survives retelling to the kettle. The script is drawn, not typed.

SUMER: THE METRONOME IS ALWAYS TICKING
STEP 2 — INK NIGHTS

Drawing goes under the tracing camera, or — only if the thriller justifies it — under the rotoscope. Ink flows at 24 frames per second, and the flow never stutters.

MUGMAN SNEEZES ONLY ONCE PER REEL
STEP 3 — PAINT MORNINGS

Cels are filled on the glass with the color wheel of 1934: five paints, two shadings, no pressed buttons on the computer. We then screen the reel for the kettle, who nods — or the kettle doesn't, in which case we redraw.

FIRST SMOKE IS ALWAYS PAINTED, NEVER GENERATED

THE HOUSE TOOLS

THE DRAWN CRAFT

Traditional 2D on real vellum — storyboards, backgrounds on cardboard, cel animation at 24 fps. Also rare feet: draftsmanship in motion sequences.

ON-HAND: 14 INKWELLS, ONE ERASER
THE PAINT, COLOR & TITLE DEPARTMENT

Hand-lettered title cards, 1934-style posters, credits laid out like theatre programmes. Paint mixing recorded in a ledger, smudges authorized only by the mentor.

COLOR WHEEL: 5 PIGMENT + 2 SHADES
THE SCREEN ROOM

Reel screening with a live kettle-jazz duo, old-style projection on real fabric. Watch parties with pencil-sticky notes — free sessions for every client.

SEATS: 9 · POPCORN: PROVIDED

ASK THE KETTLE

Hand-inked 2D shorts and title work in the visual language of 1930s Fleischer and Disney — pencil, ink, paint on cels, 24 frames per second. No compositing tricks: every frame is drawn.

About four to six studio weeks per finished minute: two weeks of boards and timing, one of ink, one of paint and titles, plus the screen-room pass with the kettle.

Both. A client picture rides the same pipeline — boards, ink, paint, screen room. The kettle grades reels we make for ourselves exactly as hard.

Five pigments and two shadings — the 1934 caps. A mix outside the ledger needs a note signed by the mentor, or the kettle sends it back to the paint desk.

The screen room seats nine. Public sessions run on Saturday pencil-days, projection on real fabric, sticky notes provided. Write a wire from the telegraph office and the kettle pencils you in.

The kettle says yes; the calendar says no. Soot Studio is a fan-fiction animation house built as a hand-inked homage — every trademark belongs to its studio, and the public domain stays public.
