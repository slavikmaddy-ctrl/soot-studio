THE ASSEMBLY LINE

FROM PENCIL TO PREMIERE

Every picture walks the same seven steps of the house. Nothing is skipped, nothing is rushed — the metronome sets the pace, the kettle keeps the score.

  1. 1

    THE GAG

    You tell the kettle your story, your audience and your deadline. We listen, ask embarrassing questions and sketch the first pencil notes the same day. No paperwork yet — just the gag, out loud.

  2. 2

    THE STORYBOARD

    Every scene becomes a thumbnail on the board — gags stacked in order, titles in the margin. You approve the flow before a single frame moves. Cheaper to move a drawing than to move a film.

  3. 3

    THE PENCIL TEST

    The boards start moving: a rough timing reel cut to the metronome. You watch the whole picture in pencil — jokes land or die here, and that is exactly where they should.

  4. 4

    INK & PAINT

    Clean lines on vellum, paint on glass, backgrounds in gouache. One honest frame after another — 24 to every second, no copying machines, no shortcuts.

  5. 5

    THE SCORE

    A jazz session for the picture — whistle-ready tunes and effects recorded the old way, on wax. The music carries the gags; the gags carry the music.

  6. 6

    THE PREMIERE

    A 24 fps master, graded and synced, in every size your screen dreams of: cinema, web, museum wall. Titles if you want them — hand-lettered, of course.

  7. 7

    THE ENCORE

    Two reels of revisions ride along free — a house rule taped above every desk. The files are yours, the original art ships in the tube, and the ink stays wet for the next picture.

HOUSE RULE №2Dailies every day — you see the picture grow, never a surprise premiere. HOUSE RULE №2424 frames per second, always. Stretch a gag to 25 and the kettle will hear of it. HOUSE RULE №7No copying machines. Every frame is drawn by a cup-headed hand.
